THRONE GAME — DESIGN NOTES (working title)
=============================================
A GoT-style political RPG where players lead noble houses vying for the
throne while a shared calamity looms over the realm.

-------------------------------------------
CORE PREMISE
-------------------------------------------
- Each player creates and plays the leader of a noble House.
- The game unfolds across DECADES (not single sessions) — a campaign can
  span generations, with characters dying/aging out and heirs stepping up.
- Win condition: the house with the HIGHEST STATS once the looming
  CALAMITY has been survived/avoided. You can't win by seizing power
  while the realm collapses — the throne only counts if the realm endures.
- The economy is ZERO-SUM: Land, Manpower, and Wealth are fixed/shared
  pools. One house's gain is another's loss. This guarantees real conflict.

-------------------------------------------
HOUSE STAT BLOCK
-------------------------------------------
Core stats (zero-sum, pooled across all houses):
  - LAND      — territory; changes hands via war, marriage, inheritance
  - MANPOWER  — fighting strength, drawn from land/population
  - WEALTH    — liquid resource for bribes, dowries, feasts, hiring
  - HONOR/INFAMY — single axis (e.g. -10 to +10), reputation.
    RESOLVED: zero-sum among houses at any given moment (same as Land/
    Manpower/Wealth), BUT random events (drawn from the same tables that
    govern Calamity/Kingdom scenes) continually add fresh Honor into an
    UNCLAIMED POOL over time, breaking the zero-sum until a house
    actively claims it (e.g. via a task, a war, a scene). So the total
    pool in play grows across the campaign even though any given moment
    is zero-sum among current holders. Same unclaimed-pool model applies
    to Land/Manpower/Wealth too. Open detail: who gets newly generated
    resources by default (split evenly? whoever the event narratively
    involves? contested via a task?) — TBD when drafting the actual
    event tables.
  - LEGEND — NEW house stat (Targaryen-inspired). Rises through
    dramatic/magical acts (winning duels others feared to attempt,
    surviving calamities, successful Magic use). RESOLVED roles:
    (1) likely the stat MAGIC tasks roll against, (2) a high Legend
    ADDS A BONUS TO THE NUMBER OF TRAITS ROLLED AT BIRTH for that
    house's children — "very few boring Targs." This creates a
    self-reinforcing dynastic engine: legendary houses produce more
    Greater (high-trait-count) heirs — more chances to roll into Magic
    — more legendary acts — Legend rises further. It also IS the
    house's risk mechanism at high values, since more traits means
    more volatility, not just more power — a high-Legend house is
    likely to keep producing magical prodigies right alongside
    burden-heavy disasters (a "Mad King" emerges from the trait math
    itself, no separate corruption roll needed). (3) likely the
    trigger stat for reputation MONIKERS (see Monikers section).
    RESOLVED — BONUS CURVE: +1 trait rolled at birth per 5 points of
    Legend (e.g. Legend 20 = +4 bonus traits). Chosen to be meaningful
    at the stat cap without being absurd.

STAT CAPS (RESOLVED): all house stats (Land, Manpower, Wealth, Legend,
etc.) soft cap around 20. Keeps dice pools rollable/fast at the table
(past ~20 dice, rolling and counting successes gets unwieldy and fights
the pacing goal) and keeps Legend's trait bonus proportionate rather
than spiraling unchecked.

Traits (house-level, all must have a real mechanical benefit AND cost —
no pure upside):
  - DYNASTIC traits — affect succession/time, e.g. Long-Lived, Fecund,
    Barren, Sickly
  - CULTURAL traits — affect specific action rolls, e.g. Great Warriors,
    Cruel, Pious, Cunning Traders. Should cut both ways (Cruel = bonus to
    intimidation/intrigue, penalty to loyalty from smallfolk).
  - HOUSE TRAIT GENERATION (RESOLVED): a house has 4 traits total at
    creation — 2 CHOSEN freely by the player + 2 ROLLED on the trait
    table. Every trait, chosen or rolled, must be given a narrative
    justification when picked.

Lineage — RESOLVED: no separate lineage system or unlock mechanic.
Lineage emerges organically — when a player picks or rolls a trait
(house or character), they must explain it narratively, and that
explanation *is* the house's lineage/history. Generated for free,
trait by trait, rather than needing its own table.

Special abilities — house-unique perks, likely tied to lineage.

Characters (individuals, i.e. the player's current leader and blood
relatives) have their own TRAITS separate from house traits. Relatives
are the pool of potential heirs and the units players delegate tasks to.

-------------------------------------------
THE DECADE LOOP (turn structure)
-------------------------------------------
1. RESOLUTION — check outcomes of last decade's tasks; roll for births/
   deaths per lineage traits; apply any trait changes.
2. SCENE DECLARATION — RESOLVED: all secrecy lives ONLY in the moment
   of commitment, not afterward. Every house writes their scene intent
   (who they want a scene with, what they're attempting — including
   poisonings, backroom deals, betrayals, alliances) on a NOTECARD, all
   cards flip/reveal SIMULTANEOUSLY. Nothing stays hidden once flipped —
   this avoids meta-gaming during the declaration itself while keeping
   everything open and GM-light afterward. No separate "secret scene"
   category, no notes held privately by the GM, no later reveal
   mechanism needed.
   - Number of scenes scales with player count.
3. SCENE RESOLUTION — since nothing is hidden after declaration, all
   scenes can run in parallel around the table with no GM bottleneck.
4. KINGDOM SCENE — GM reveals the decade's big public moment. Drawn
   randomly or pulled from an ongoing plotline. No longer needs to
   double as a "secret reveal" valve — it's free to just be the big
   story set-piece on its own terms.
5. TASK ASSIGNMENT — players delegate blood relatives to new tasks for
   the coming decade, based on relative traits + house stats.

-------------------------------------------
ACTION / TASK TYPES
-------------------------------------------
Candidates (each ties to 1-2 house stats + the acting relative's traits):
  - WAR
  - TRADE
  - DIPLOMACY
  - ESPIONAGE
  - STEWARDSHIP (managing own land/manpower growth)
  - FAITH (maybe)

-------------------------------------------
RESOLUTION MECHANIC
-------------------------------------------
- Base roll: POOL OF d6s. Count 5-6 as a success.
- Pool size = relevant house stat + relative's trait bonus (if trait
  fits the task) + scene modifiers.
- Opposed tasks: compare pools/successes directly (e.g. Espionage vs.
  Stewardship defense).
- Zero-sum tie-in: War task outcomes directly transfer Land/Manpower/
  Wealth dice from the losing house's pool to the winner's for the next
  decade, so pools visibly shrink/grow as the game progresses.

CHECK TYPES (RESOLVED — two distinct resolution modes):
- STATIC CHECKS — used when claiming from the UNCLAIMED POOL or acting
  on the world/circumstance rather than against another house (e.g.
  Stewardship, Trade, claiming unclaimed Land). Rolled against a fixed
  target number. PURE PASS/FAIL — no margin grading, no partial
  success. Simple and fast, since these aren't inherently dramatic
  interpersonal conflicts.
- COMPETITIVE CHECKS — opposed rolls directly against another house
  (War, contested claims, duels). GRADED VICTORY based on margin of
  successes:
    - Win by MORE THAN 1 success — decisive win, CAPTURE the resource
      directly from the opponent's house.
    - Win by EXACTLY 1 success — narrow win, the resource does NOT
      transfer to you — it goes to the UNCLAIMED POOL instead. You won,
      but not decisively enough to claim the spoils outright.
    - Lose — you get nothing (and may lose resources yourself,
      depending on the task's stakes).
  Rationale: keeps interpersonal conflict from being purely predatory
  (a narrow win doesn't let you strip a rival bare), gives losers a
  reason to still contest even at bad odds (forcing a narrow win denies
  the winner the spoils), and gives the unclaimed pool another feed
  source beyond random events. Open: sanity-check the margin-of-1
  threshold against real dice math once a War task is playtested — if
  wins by exactly 1 are rare/common at typical pool sizes, the rule may
  need tuning.

-------------------------------------------
DESTINY (single-use, not a resource pool)
-------------------------------------------
- Not a spendable currency/score — a single-use FLAG. A house either
  holds Destiny or doesn't. Only ONE per house at a time.
- Effect when spent: ALL 6s in the pool explode (reroll and keep adding),
  not just one die — bigger pools = more explosive potential.
- Earned by (must be an EVENT/CHANGE, not a static state):
  - The leader of a house dies — the new heir inherits Destiny
  - A house OVERTAKES the current #1 in some stat/ranking (not for
    just being #1 — must be the moment of dethroning)
  - A house BECOMES the new lowest-ranked (displacing prior bottom
    house — not for sitting at the bottom)
  - A calamity roll (plague, fire, ruin) grants it as compensation
  - Resolving a vendetta against another house (winning a duel, winning
    a hand in marriage, etc.)
  - Winning the crown (see King/Revolt below)
- Design intent: Destiny rewards PEAKS, VALLEYS, and MOMENTS OF CHANGE —
  not stability. Nobody in the comfortable middle gets it.

-------------------------------------------
KING & REVOLT
-------------------------------------------
- A King is elected from among the houses; wins Destiny upon election.
- The King's house draws from a HARSHER calamity table than other
  houses (and/or other houses get bonus odds when scheming/warring
  against the King's house specifically) — "heavy is the head that
  wears the crown."
- The King remains King until:
  a) Their house falls/collapses entirely, or
  b) A REVOLT is triggered.
- This makes the crown self-destabilizing: no house can camp the throne
  indefinitely, since holding it actively increases the odds of losing
  it.

ELECTION/REVOLT TRIGGER (RESOLVED — reactive, not scheduled):
- No recurring election cycle. A house (or houses) DECLARES a bid to
  contest the crown during Scene Declaration (same notecard/simultaneous
  mechanism as regular scenes). This resolves at the NEXT Kingdom scene.
- FAILURE RISK for a challenger: the challenging leader's life is at
  risk, AND a quarter of their house's stats return to the central
  unclaimed pool. Reaching for the crown and missing can badly cripple a
  dynasty, not just embarrass it.

ALLIANCES & RESOLUTION (RESOLVED):
- At the Kingdom scene where the Revolt resolves, ALLIANCES ARE DRAWN
  live — any houses (challengers or loyalists) can band together into a
  bloc, pooling their combined stats into one shared pool for the roll.
- The King's house is NOT passive — they actively gather LOYALIST houses
  into their own defending bloc during this same phase, mirroring the
  challengers. This means the King's past behavior (pool grants given,
  redistributions taken) directly determines how many loyalists show up
  — generosity and coercion as King are literally banking future support
  for the moment a Revolt happens.
- Resulting blocs (however many form) roll against each other for the
  crown.
- SUCCESSION WHEN A COALITION WINS: not mechanically resolved. The
  winning houses must agree amongst themselves who actually takes the
  crown. If they can't agree (or the deal falls apart), that can itself
  spiral into a FOLLOWING REVOLT. Left to table negotiation/roleplay on
  purpose — the coalition falling apart after winning is meant to be a
  story beat, not a rule.

KING'S POWERS (per turn, RESOLVED — fixed sequence, not a free choice):
1. MUST draw first: grant 1 unit of any resource (Wealth, Manpower,
   etc.) to any 1 house, drawn from the shared UNCLAIMED POOL (costs the
   King's own house nothing).
2. THEN, additionally, may take 1 unit of a resource from one house and
   give it to another (redistribution) — a sharper, more coercive tool
   that almost certainly generates friction with the house it's taken
   from. This is a natural, built-in Revolt-generator: an aggressive
   King manufactures the grievances that lead to their own downfall,
   without needing an extra rule to force it.
- MERCY: the King may choose to SPARE a defeated rebel house instead of
  enforcing the failure cost (leader's life / quarter-stats-to-pool).
  RESOLVED: mercy is mechanically FREE — no stat cost, no forced Infamy
  change. The cost lives entirely at the TABLE, not on the sheet: it's
  a visible political choice other players will remember and factor
  into their own future alliances/Revolt calculations. Consistent with
  how Honor/Infamy already work — consequence lives in play, not in a
  forced number.

-------------------------------------------
CHARACTER GENERATION (births)
-------------------------------------------
Rolled during the RESOLUTION phase whenever a birth occurs.

- TRAIT COUNT — roll a number of traits between 2 and 7 for the newborn.
  This count alone determines LESSER vs. GREATER status; there is no
  separate Lesser/Greater roll, it's just how many traits you get.
  - IMPORTANT: Greater does NOT mean better. More traits just means a
    more DEFINED and VOLATILE character — more likely to roll both
    strong boons and real liabilities (Cruel, Sickly, Cowardly, Cursed,
    etc. are just as available as Great Warrior). A Lesser character
    with 2 clean traits can be a safer bet than a Greater one dragging
    several burdens alongside their gifts.
  - Trait table is MIXED (boons and burdens together, not split into
    separate tables) — any roll can land either way.
- EACH TRAIT SLOT — roll individually on the (mixed) trait table.
- MAGIC — extremely rare. No separate roll; instead, a chosen MAGICAL
  HOUSE TRAIT lets the player replace any individual trait roll of X-or-
  higher with Magic instead, when it comes up.
  - Side effect (intended): Greater characters roll more trait slots,
    so they naturally get more chances to hit the magic threshold —
    magic is more likely to "find" exceptional (high trait count)
    characters, without that being an explicit separate rule. Now
    reinforced further by LEGEND (see house stat block) increasing
    trait counts for legendary houses.
  - The magical house trait itself should carry a real cost proportional
    to how much it bends the odds (candidates: Infamy, drawing Calamity
    attention, straining Honor) — magic being rare and powerful is the
    point, so a trait that makes it common needs to hurt.
  - RESOLVED — conversion is a PLAYER CHOICE, not automatic. Even with
    a qualifying roll, the player decides whether to take Magic or keep
    what they rolled — different from burden traits like Cursed, which
    just land on you with no opt-out. Reinforces that Magic is
    something a house deliberately reaches for.
  - RESOLVED — mechanical effect: Magic does NOT grant bonus dice like
    a cultural trait. Instead it UNLOCKS UNIQUE TASK OPTIONS other
    characters simply cannot attempt at all (e.g. scrying, cursing a
    rival's harvest, communing with something not fully of this world).
    Keeps Magic feeling exceptional rather than a stacked stat bonus.
  - RESOLVED — magical tasks use the SAME d6 pool / count-successes
    mechanic as everything else, just gated behind having the Magic
    trait to attempt them. Likely rolls against LEGEND (see house stat
    block) rather than a standard task stat — not fully locked.

-------------------------------------------
HONOR / INFAMY — CLARIFICATION
-------------------------------------------
- Honor/Infamy is NOT rolled and not tied to traits. Traits describe
  CAPABILITY and DISPOSITION (Great Warrior, Cunning, Cruel, Silver-
  Tongued) — they are morally neutral. Honor/Infamy is earned entirely
  through PLAY — how a player actually uses those capabilities in scenes
  and tasks (a Great Warrior trait doesn't make you honorable OR
  villainous — using it to defend the realm vs. slaughter a wedding
  party does).
- This keeps the trait table free of needing separate "good" and "evil"
  trait categories.

-------------------------------------------
MONIKERS
-------------------------------------------
- Two flavors under consideration:
  - PHYSICAL monikers ("the Tall," "the One-Eyed," "the Fair") — could
    be a birth-roll table alongside traits.
  - REPUTATION monikers ("the Butcher," "the Just," "the Kingslayer") —
    EARNED through play, not rolled at birth.
- DECISION: Only the HOUSE LEADER can earn a reputation moniker, and
  only through actions (not rolled at birth). This keeps monikers
  meaningful and marks which characters in a house's lineage actually
  mattered — a chronicle of leaders could read "Aldric the Just, then
  Wren the Cruel, then Tomas who was never named," which says something
  on its own.
- Open: is a moniker awarded at GM discretion based on how a Kingdom
  scene/major action played out, or tied to specific mechanical
  triggers (e.g. win 3 War tasks — unlock a "Conqueror"-tier moniker
  pool; hit very negative Infamy — unlock a "Cruel"-tier pool)? Leaning
  toward mechanical triggers to stay consistent with how Destiny and
  other systems in this game are dice/table-driven rather than pure GM
  fiat.

-------------------------------------------
PACING
-------------------------------------------
- RESOLVED: sessions run a couple hours and should cover roughly 30-80
  years — i.e. 3-8 decades per session (a generation or two). This means
  each full decade loop (Resolution — Scene Declaration — Scene
  Resolution — Kingdom Scene — Task Assignment) needs to move fast,
  targeting roughly 15-25 minutes per decade. Reinforces earlier calls:
  flat d6 pools over heavier resolution, notecard scenes should stay
  quick to write/resolve rather than long negotiations every decade.

-------------------------------------------
DESIGN PRINCIPLES CARRIED FROM THIS DISCUSSION
-------------------------------------------
- All traits must have both a mechanical benefit AND a cost — no pure
  upside traits. This is what keeps house identity feeling like a
  narrative choice rather than a build-optimization exercise.
- Zero-sum economy is core to guaranteeing real player-vs-player
  conflict — no house can grow in isolation.
- Chosen/parallel scenes avoid GM bottlenecks; secret scenes are the
  one category that need GM involvement (private info).
- The throne and the calamity are fused into one win condition on
  purpose — you can't "win" by seizing power while the realm burns.
- Destiny should stay a rare, dramatic trigger tied to CHANGE (death,
  overtaking, falling, calamity, crowning) — not a passive stat-based
  bonus.
