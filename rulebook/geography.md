# Geography — House Order as the Map of the Realm

House order (established at setup, see `setup-order.md`) isn't just a
scene-conflict tiebreaker — it's a **ring**, and it doubles as the map of
the realm.

- Houses sit in a circle. The King's seat is **the capital**; every
  house's distance from that seat represents its in-fiction distance
  from the capital.
- Every house has exactly **2 neighbors** — whichever houses sit
  immediately to its left and right in the ring. Those are the only
  houses it shares a border with.
- The King's house keeps its normal position in the ring like anyone
  else, with its own 2 ordinary neighbors. Being the capital is flavor
  for where the seat currently sits, not a mechanical hub — it doesn't
  grant the King's house extra war targets, and doesn't protect it from
  any either.

## Border wars
**The War task can only target an adjacent house** — one of your 2
current ring-neighbors. You cannot declare War on a house you don't
share a border with, no matter how far away or how enticing a target
they are.

This is deliberately scoped to War only. Trade, Diplomacy, Espionage,
Stewardship, and Magic are all unrestricted by geography — coin, words,
spies, and rituals aren't blocked by distance the way armies are. A
house on the far side of the ring is safe from your armies, but not from
your spies or your marriage proposals.

## The King redraws the map
This is what gives real teeth to the King's per-turn power to move 1
house's position in the ring (see `turn-order.md`, Phase 4): moving a
house doesn't just change scene-conflict priority anymore, it **changes
who can go to war with whom**. A King can isolate a rival by moving them
away from an ally who might reinforce them, or force two feuding houses
into forced neighboring adjacency. The crown quite literally draws the
borders.

## When a house falls
If a house falls/collapses entirely, it's removed from the ring and the
**gap closes** — its two former neighbors become newly adjacent to each
other, same as if the fallen house had never been there.

**The player is not out of the campaign.** They create a brand new house
using the exact same procedure as any house at setup (`setup-order.md`,
steps 2-5: full 12-point resource allocation, house traits, House Leader
+ family via Birth Deal) — no restart penalty, no reduced budget. The
new house is inserted into the ring **adjacent to a house of the
player's choosing**, expanding the ring by one seat at that point. This
keeps every player engaged for the whole campaign rather than sitting
out for however many decades/sessions remain.

---

The initial ring arrangement is pure table negotiation — players sort
out who sits where at setup, no roll or fixed rule involved.
