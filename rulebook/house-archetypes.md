# House Archetype & Succession Custom

Two choices made once at setup (see `setup-order.md`), alongside the 4
house traits — foundational identity, not something rolled or changed
later.

## House Archetype
What kind of institution this house actually is. All seven require
some real, official standing in the realm — the table considered and
rejected archetypes without legitimate recognition (a criminal
syndicate, an unsanctioned raider fleet).

Every non-baseline archetype gets one small dice bonus and one small
dice cost, same "real benefit, real cost" principle as house traits —
this is a foundational choice, not a free upgrade.

- **Noble House** — the default. Landed, hereditary, no special rule —
  this is what the rest of the game already assumes. No bonus, no cost.
- **Merchant House** — wealth over land. +1 die on Trade actions. -1
  die on War actions — underinvests in martial strength.
- **Mercenary Company** — a war-band, not a dynasty. +1 die on War
  actions. -1 die on Stewardship actions — built to move, not to
  administer land.
- **Priestly Cloister** — faith over blood. +1 die on Invoke Faith and
  Declare Heresy specifically. -1 die on Trade actions — devoted to
  faith, not profit. Also gains access to **Ordain a Novice** (see
  below) as an additional way to gain a relative, alongside normal
  births — doesn't replace the Fate Check, just adds a second path.
- **Magical Order** — bound by Legend and Magic, not land or lineage.
  Automatically has the effects of the **Magical** house trait (see
  `tables/house-traits.md`, Spades Q) without spending one of the 4
  trait picks on it. -1 die on War actions — mages, not soldiers.
- **Knightly Order** — oath-bound military service, not bloodline. +1
  die on War actions. -1 die on Trade actions — knights serve, they
  don't haggle.
- **Scholarly Order** — knowledge and administration over land or
  blood. +1 die on Stewardship actions. -1 die on Diplomacy actions —
  secluded scholars, poor at courtly politics.

### Ordain a Novice (Priestly Cloister only)
A new Diplomacy-category action, STATIC, **Wealth + Land** (same combo
as Invoke Faith). Success: generates a new relative via Birth Deal,
starting at **age 2** (an adult convert, not a newborn) rather than age
0. Doesn't touch the Fate Check's birth mechanic — a Cloister can still
have ordinary births too, this is purely an additional growth path.

## Succession Custom
Determines who becomes the new Leader when the old one dies, by any
means (Fate Check, War Death Check, Assassination, etc.). Chosen once
at setup, applies for the whole campaign. All four are fully
deterministic — at any point in play, the table can look at the family
tree and know exactly who the current heir is, no election or contest
needed.

- **Primogeniture** — the eldest living child inherits, regardless of
  gender.
- **Patriarchal** — the eldest living SON inherits; if there are no
  living sons, the eldest living daughter inherits instead.
- **Matriarchal** — the eldest living DAUGHTER inherits; if there are
  no living daughters, the eldest living son inherits instead.
- **Ultimogeniture** — the YOUNGEST living child inherits.

Succession custom is independent of archetype — a Mercenary Company can
still be strictly Primogeniture if that's how leadership has settled
for them; the two choices don't force each other.

**If no qualifying child is alive when the Leader dies**, the house has
no heir — this is the EXTINCTION collapse condition already defined in
`birth-death.md`, unaffected by which custom was chosen. A surviving
spouse does NOT inherit under any of the four customs — they're consort,
not blood heir, so a childless widow/widower doesn't continue the
house's leadership line. *(Flagging this as a real design choice, not
an oversight — happy to add a regency/spousal exception if it feels too
harsh at the table.)*

A very young heir (e.g. an infant under Ultimogeniture, or any custom
if the only qualifying child is newborn) simply becomes the new Leader
outright — no formal regency mechanic. Narrate it as guided by family
elders until they're grown; nothing mechanically different happens.
