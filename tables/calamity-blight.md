# Calamity Module: Blight

**STATUS: DRAFTED.** Third of the 4 Calamity modules, built on the
shared endgame shape — see `tables/calamity-shared-endgame.md` for the
mechanics common to every module (Competitive-vs-Calamity, decade×
trial scaling, alliance, the standard decisive/narrow/loss shape, the
survivor score tally). This file only covers what's unique to Blight:
its theme, Doom Clock trigger, prep action, and its 3 trials.

## Theme
Nothing arrives all at once. Crops come in a little thinner each
harvest. Wells run a little more bitter. Livestock sicken for no
reason anyone can name, and the old stories about the dark between the
stars stop sounding like old stories. The blight isn't an army or a
rival sorcerer — it's an absence, spreading wherever faith and
devotion aren't actively holding it back. Light, in this module, isn't
metaphorical. It's Piety, spent and renewed, standing between the
realm and the slow dark.

## The Doom Clock
Starts at **0**, maxes at **10**.

**Advance trigger:** checked once per decade, at Kingdom Scene — **if
no house at the table has Piety 5 or higher, the Doom Clock advances
by 1.** One sufficiently devout house is enough to hold the dark back
for everyone that decade; the realm doesn't need every house to be
pious, just needs SOMEONE to be. If every house lets its faith lapse
at once, the blight advances.

## Preparing for the End: Kindle the Ward-Light
A new **Diplomacy** action, **STATIC, Piety + Land** — a third
deliberate exception to "passive stats aren't rolled" (joining Declare
Heresy and all of Magic, see `tasks.md`). Attemptable **once per
decade**, any decade from the moment the module is chosen. A house
already devout has an easier time consecrating its grounds than one
starting from nothing — Piety invested through Invoke Faith pays
forward here.

- **Success:** bank **1 Beacon**. A house may hold at most **3
  Beacons** at once — one per trial.
- **Failure:** the rite gutters out, and doubt creeps in where it was
  meant to keep faith burning — **lose 1 Piety.** Its own distinct
  cost: not a resource drain like Invasion's failed muster, not
  feeding the Clock like Magic-Born's failed ritual — a failed
  consecration costs the very faith it was trying to build.

**Spending Beacons, during the Long Dark:** after a trial's roll
resolves, spend any number of banked Beacons to upgrade that trial's
outcome one tier per Beacon spent (Loss→Narrow win, Narrow win→
Decisive win). Same rule as Wards and Bulwarks — they soften the
blow, they don't guarantee anything, and a house that let its faith
lapse across the whole campaign has no light left to spend when the
dark finally arrives in full.

## The Final Round: The Long Dark
When the Doom Clock strikes its final mark, whatever faith was left
holding it back gives out all at once. Uses the standard Final Round
mechanics (see `calamity-shared-endgame.md`): 3 sequential Competitive
trials against a scaling Calamity pool, alliance allowed, every death
check one age-tier worse, Ruin/Extinction knocks a house out of
contention immediately.

### Trial 1: The Withering — Land + Wealth
The harvest fails first, and then the next, and then there isn't a
harvest at all — just fields the color of old bone. What a house can
still buy or trade for is the only thing standing between its people
and the famine underneath everything else.

- **Decisive win:** enough is scraped together to hold — but not for
  free. Lose **1 Wealth**.
- **Narrow win:** lose **1 Land and 1 Wealth**, and the player's
  choice of ONE relative takes a death check.
- **Loss:** standard Loss (see `calamity-shared-endgame.md`) — lose 5
  from every stat, every relative takes a death check. The withering
  doesn't spare what a house is proud of any more than what it needs
  to survive.

### Trial 2: The Long Despair — Piety + Honor/Infamy
This is the trial no army or treasury can fight. The dark doesn't
break down gates — it breaks down belief. What holds a house's people
together when the sky itself feels like it's failing isn't steel or
coin, it's whether they still trust the name over the gate — feared or
beloved, it doesn't matter which, only that it's believed in. Roll
Piety + this house's Honor/Infamy, counted as its **absolute
magnitude** (a house that inspires through fear holds its people
together the same as one that inspires through love; only a house
nobody has strong feelings about at all has nothing to rally them).

- **Decisive win:** the people hold, and so does the house — but faith
  spent is faith spent. Lose **1 Piety**.
- **Narrow win:** lose **1 Piety**, and this house's Honor/Infamy
  shifts **1 point toward neutral** (whichever pole it currently
  leans), and ONE chosen relative takes a death check — the despair
  finds someone, even in a house that mostly held together.
- **Loss:** standard Loss — when belief breaks, everything built on it
  goes with it.

### Trial 3: The Rot Made Flesh — Manpower + Piety
What the blight leaves behind doesn't stay in the ground. Something
wears the shape of what used to graze the fields, or till them, or
live in the villages nearby — and it has to be met with real steel,
carried by soldiers who still believe there's something worth
defending.

- **Decisive win:** it's put down, at a cost — lose **1 Manpower**.
- **Narrow win:** lose **1 Manpower and 1 Piety**, and ONE chosen
  relative takes a death check.
- **Loss:** standard Loss — soldiers without faith break as easily
  against the rot as against despair, and this house has run out of
  both.

Losing Trial 1 and Trial 3 together already guarantees full Ruin
(standard Loss zeroes Wealth, Land, and Manpower between them)
regardless of Trial 2's result — and Trial 2's own Loss, on top of
either, means the house's people no longer believe in it at all.
**There is no version of losing the whole Long Dark — or even losing
badly partway through it — that leaves a house quietly surviving.**
