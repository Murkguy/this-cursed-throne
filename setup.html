How to Start

Follow these seven steps in order. Every step is a resolved rule — nothing here should require the table to guess.

No GM

This game has no persistent Game Master. Every check resolves by fixed rule — a dice pool, a card flip, or a table lookup — never by adjudication. During play, the King's player hosts the Kingdom Scene reveal (see Phase 4); before any King exists, any player can handle purely mechanical bookkeeping like Decade 1's forced draw. It never matters who physically flips a card — the fixed rule decides the outcome either way.

1. Gather players

Each player creates and leads one noble House for the campaign.

  • Minimum 4 players. No upper cap — table size is the players' own call. Scene count scales automatically with player count (one Scene per House per decade), so more players just means more Scenes running in parallel, not more per House.
  • Establish a house order now — sort out among yourselves who sits where in a ring. No roll, no fixed rule, pure table negotiation. This is also the map of the realm — the ring determines who can go to War with whom, and it only changes later when a King spends their per-turn power to move a House.

2. Choose a Calamity module

Before anything else about the world is decided, the table picks one Calamity module together — the shared threat the campaign is building toward. Each module defines its own Doom Clock length, its own advance trigger, its own prep action, and its own Final Round. See Calamity Modules for full detail.

3. Create your House

3a. House Archetype & Succession Custom

Pick a House Archetype and a Succession Custom — both one-time choices for the whole campaign. See House Archetypes for the full list and what each does. Done first because some archetypes affect the next two steps (Magical Order auto-grants the Magical trait; Golden affects your resource budget).

3b. Generate house traits

Every House has 4 traits total: 2 chosen freely by the player, 2 rolled on the House Trait table (flip 1 card, look up its exact suit+rank directly, no math). Every trait — chosen or rolled — needs a narrative justification when picked; that justification is the House's lineage. Done before resource allocation, since one trait (Golden) changes the budget in step 3c.

3c. Allocate starting resources

Divide 12 points (or 14, with Golden) among Land, Manpower, and Wealth — minimum 1 in each. E.g. a war-first House might go 8/2/2; a balanced House might go 4/4/4. Each House gets the same budget, allocated independently.

4. Create your House Leader

Generated with the same Birth Deal procedure used for any birth during play — no special case for the starting Leader. Starts at age 3 (30–39 years old).

1Roll 10d6, counting successes on 5–6. Add automatic hits equal to the House's Legend ÷ 5, rounded down. No floor, no cap — a Legend-0 House can rarely roll as few as 0 traits; a high-Legend House can roll well past what the old system ever allowed.
2Flip that many cards; each card's exact suit+rank is a direct lookup on the 52-entry Character Trait table.
3An Ace of Spades is always eligible for Magic conversion; if the House has the Magical trait, any face card is also eligible. Conversion is a player choice.

5. Create your Leader's starting family

Every House begins with a spouse and at least 1 child, in addition to the Leader.

  • Spouse: generated via the same Birth Deal procedure. May start at a different age than the Leader if narratively justified (an older political match, a young second marriage) — otherwise assumed to match.
  • Child(ren): exactly 1, via Birth Deal — unless the House took Fecund, which starts it with 1–2 extra children instead (flip 1 card: Ace–6 → 1 extra, 7–King → 2 extra). Each extra child is individually generated via Birth Deal.

6. Confirm the throne is vacant

No House starts as King. But the throne cannot stay empty past Decade 1: if any House bids during Decade 1's Scene Declaration, it resolves normally as a Revolt. If nobody bids, any player flips one card per House at Decade 1's Kingdom Scene — highest card is forced onto the throne (redraw ties). The curse doesn't wait for volunteers.

7. Begin play

Proceed to the first decade's Resolution phase. See Playing a Decade for the full loop, and a worked example across five decades of play.
