Rules Reference

The full rulebook in one place — stats, checks, every task action, births and deaths, the map, House identity, and the throne.

Active vs. Passive stats

The house stat block splits into two groups:

  • ACTIVE stats — rolled in Task actions: Land, Manpower, Wealth, Cunning.
  • PASSIVE stats — never rolled: Honor/Infamy, Piety, Legend. Checked by events for who's highest/lowest, rewarded or punished accordingly — never something a player actively spends dice on.
Task categories are not stats

War, Trade, Diplomacy, Espionage, Stewardship, and Magic are groupings of actions, not rollable values. Any opposition always names the actual stat.

Two explicit exceptions to "passive stats are never rolled": Declare Heresy uses Piety, and all of Magic uses Legend — both thematically unique enough to justify breaking the rule on purpose.

Cunning starts at 3 for every House — a small flat value, not part of the 12-point budget, so Espionage isn't nearly impossible early on. Grows through play: a decisive win on any Espionage or Border Raid action grants +1 Cunning.

Combo actions add both listed stats together into one bigger pool.

Static vs. Competitive

Every die in a pool succeeds on a 5 or 6. Both check types use that same base roll — they differ in what counts as success.

STATIC

Pure pass/fail, no margin grading. Succeed if successes are at least round(pool ÷ 3) + 1. This target scales with pool size so the odds stay roughly 1-in-3 whether you're rolling 2 dice or 20 — a pool of 6 needs 3 successes (~32%), a pool of 15 needs 6 (~38%). Tension comes from which Task slot to spend, not from padding the pool.

COMPETITIVE

Opposed roll — both sides roll their pool, compare successes. The margin decides the outcome, using a band width that scales with the larger pool involved:

  • Pool under 5 dice → any win at all is decisive (no denial band)
  • Pool 5–13 → decisive needs a margin of 2+
  • Pool 14+ → decisive needs a margin of 3+

A margin inside the denial band is a narrow win; losing (or tying, where an action calls it out) is a loss. Each action below spells out its own decisive/narrow/loss outcomes.

War — Manpower-based

Always targets a ring-adjacent house.

  • Border Raid — one-off strike. COMPETITIVE, Manpower+Cunning vs. the target's Land. Stakes: 1 Wealth — decisive win captures it, narrow win destroys it, loss gets nothing. No death check.
  • War — a standing state, not one-off. Declaring it resolves an immediate battle: COMPETITIVE, Manpower+Wealth vs. the same combo. Stakes: 1 Land AND 1 Manpower — decisive win captures both, narrow win sends both to the unclaimed pool, loss gets nothing. The declaring relative takes a War Death Check. Once declared, it continues automatically every decade until either house resolves Broker Peace.

Trade — Wealth-based

  • Broker Deal — STATIC, Wealth+Cunning. Claim unclaimed Wealth from the pool.
  • Trade War — COMPETITIVE, Wealth+Cunning vs. same. Decisive win captures 1 Wealth from the target; narrow win destroys it instead; loss gets nothing. Not restricted by geography.
  • Renegotiate — NO ROLL. A cooperative deal between two willing, ring-adjacent houses — exchange any amount of Land/Manpower/Wealth at whatever rate they agree. Doesn't touch the unclaimed pool.

Diplomacy — mixed

The most roleplay-forward category — most of the actual work happens in the Scene itself; the roll only matters for specific, concrete outcomes.

  • Arrange Marriage — STATIC, Wealth+Land. Only rolled for a foreign marriage (into/from another player House). Success: spouse generated via Birth Deal, both houses gain +2 dice acting together going forward, then flip 1 card for a dowry (Queen or King transfers 1 Wealth). Failure: no marriage.
  • Forge Alliance — STATIC, Manpower+Cunning. Same lasting +2-dice bond as Arrange Marriage, without a marriage.
  • Court Favor — STATIC, Cunning alone. Claim unclaimed Honor from the pool.
  • Invoke Faith — STATIC, Wealth+Land. Claim unclaimed Piety from the pool.
  • Declare Heresy — COMPETITIVE, Piety+2 (flat) vs. same. Decisive win: target branded heretic, -2 Diplomacy dice until cleared. Narrow win: same penalty but only until the target's next Diplomacy attempt. Loss: it backfires — the accuser takes the full indefinite penalty instead.
  • Broker Peace — NO ROLL. Ends a vendetta or standing War peacefully. Grants +1 Honor, can trigger Destiny.

Espionage — Cunning-based

Always COMPETITIVE, opposed by the target's Land.

  • Assassinate — Cunning+Manpower, targets a specific relative. Fixed breakpoints, not scaled by pool size:
    • Win by any margin: the relative dies cleanly, assassin undiscovered.
    • Tie: the relative dies, but the assassin is discovered and captured.
    • Lose by 1: fails cleanly, assassin escapes.
    • Lose by 2+: fails, and the assassin is discovered and captured.
    Captured ≠ dead — a real narrative complication (ransom, hostage, execution), not an automatic death check.
  • Steal Secrets — Cunning alone. Decisive win: pick one of the target's passive stats and reduce it by 2. Narrow win: reduce by 1. Loss: nothing.
  • Sow Rumors — Cunning+Wealth. Always Honor/Infamy specifically. Decisive win: target's Honor/Infamy shifts 2 toward Infamy. Narrow win: shifts 1. Loss: backfires — the attacker shifts 1 toward Infamy instead.

Stewardship — Land-based

Always STATIC. Never initiates a Competitive check, but Land is what a house rolls defensively against Espionage or a Border Raid.

  • Develop Territory — Land+Manpower. Claim unclaimed Land, Wealth, OR Manpower (your choice).
  • Fortify — Land+Wealth. No claim — instead, +2 dice banked for your next Espionage or Border Raid defense.

Magic — Legend-based

Every Magic action uses Legend+Cunning — the second explicit exception to "passive stats aren't rolled." Always STATIC unless noted. Gated at the character level: only a relative who underwent Magic conversion during Birth Deal can attempt a Magic action — having the Magical house trait widens the odds of conversion, it doesn't let every relative cast.

  • Scry — Legend+Cunning. Peek at the next card of any deck before it's drawn.
  • Curse — COMPETITIVE, Legend+Cunning vs. same. Decisive win: target draws 2 keep worst on their next TWO relevant draws. Narrow win: same, next ONE draw. Loss: backfires on the caster instead.
  • Bless — Legend+Cunning, no opposed roll, targets another house only. Draws 2 keep best on their next relevant draw, +1 Piety.

Each Calamity module also grants exactly one dedicated prep action for its own ending — see Calamity Modules.

Birth & Death — The Fate Check

Runs every decade, Resolution Phase 1, step 1 — the ambient background life-cycle mechanic. Only the House Leader, spouse, and direct descendants (and their own spouses/children, if developed) are tracked.

Age is measured in decades since birth (age 0 = born this decade, 10 years per point). The House Leader starts at age 3 (30–39). A spouse may start at a different age if narratively justified.

AgeDeath bandApprox. odds
0–2 (0–29 yrs)Ace~7.7%
3–5 (30–59 yrs)Ace–2~15.4%
6–8 (60–89 yrs)Ace–4~30.8%
9+ (90+ yrs)Ace–7~53.8%

One card flip per relative per decade — death occurs if the rank falls in-band. Sickly shifts one tier older; Long-Lived shifts one tier younger (floor at tier 0). Median death age ~50 years; Sickly drags it to ~33, Long-Lived extends it to ~68.

War Death Check: a relative assigned to War gets one additional death check when that task's outcome is determined — win or lose.

Birth Check

One flip per viable couple per decade, independent of the death check. Birth occurs on rank Queen–King (~15.4%), not age-weighted. Fecund widens the range to 9–King (~30.8%). A birth generates the child immediately via Birth Deal, age 0.

House Collapse

  • Extinction: the House Leader dies with no living heir to inherit.
  • Ruin: Land, Manpower, AND Wealth all hit 0 simultaneously — not any one alone, so a low-Land/high-Wealth merchant house is safe.

A fallen house is removed from the ring (the gap closes), but the player is never eliminated — they build a fresh house via the identical setup procedure, inserted into the ring adjacent to a house of their choosing.

Geography — The Ring

House order isn't just a scene-conflict tiebreaker — it's a ring, and it's the map of the realm. Houses sit in a circle; the King's seat is the capital (flavor only, not a mechanical hub). Every house has exactly 2 neighbors.

War can only target an adjacent house. Trade, Diplomacy, Espionage, Stewardship, and Magic are all unrestricted by geography.

The King's per-turn power to move 1 house's ring position has real teeth here — it changes who can go to war with whom, not just scene priority. The crown quite literally draws the borders.

House Archetypes

What kind of institution a house actually is — a one-time choice, made at setup alongside Succession Custom. Every non-baseline archetype gets one small dice bonus and one small dice cost.

ArchetypeBonusCost
Noble HouseThe default — no bonus, no cost.
Merchant House+1 die Trade-1 die War
Mercenary Company+1 die War-1 die Stewardship
Priestly Cloister+1 die Invoke Faith & Declare Heresy; gains Ordain a Novice-1 die Trade
Magical OrderAuto-grants the Magical house trait, free (5th trait)-1 die War
Knightly Order+1 die War-1 die Trade
Scholarly Order+1 die Stewardship-1 die Diplomacy

Ordain a Novice (Priestly Cloister only) — new Diplomacy action, STATIC, Wealth+Land. Success generates a relative via Birth Deal at age 2 (an adult convert), an additional growth path alongside ordinary births.

Succession Custom

Determines the new Leader when the old one dies, by any means. Chosen once at setup. All four are fully deterministic — no election, no contest.

  • Primogeniture — eldest living child, any gender.
  • Patriarchal — eldest living son; eldest daughter if no sons.
  • Matriarchal — eldest living daughter; eldest son if no daughters.
  • Ultimogeniture — youngest living child.

No qualifying heir = Extinction, regardless of custom. A surviving spouse never inherits under any custom — confirmed final, no regency exception.

Becoming King

Exactly two paths to the throne: winning a Revolt (any decade after the first), or Decade 1's forced draw if nobody bids.

Either way, becoming King grants immediately and permanently: +10 Legend (never reverts — the curse's mark) and 1 Destiny.

Declaring a Revolt

Any house may bid during Scene Declaration — uses that house's one scene slot. No ring-adjacency requirement. Resolves at that same decade's Kingdom Scene.

Alliances

Drawn live at Kingdom Scene. The King's house auto-anchors the loyalist bloc. Each bidder anchors their own challenger bloc. Any other house may join any bloc, or abstain. Each house is represented by its Leader (automatically committed); a player may nominate additional relatives to share the risk.

The Roll

COMPETITIVE, binary win/lose — no narrow-win distinction. Each bloc's pool = sum of every member house's Manpower+Wealth, plus flat trait bonuses. Highest successes wins the crown. Ties redraw among only the tied blocs.

Outcomes

Winning bloc takes the throne — a single house's Leader becomes King immediately; a coalition must agree among themselves (not mechanically resolved — a fractured deal can spiral into a following Revolt).

Every losing bloc pays the same failure cost, including a dethroned King's house: 1/4 of Land, Manpower, and Wealth (rounded down, each stat separately) to the unclaimed pool, and every relative committed to that bloc's contribution takes a death check.

Mercy: the winning bloc's Leader may spare any specific losing house from the failure cost entirely — free, available to whichever side wins, granted per-house.

Destiny

A single-use flag, not a resource pool. A house either holds it or doesn't — only ONE at a time (Ambitious is the sole exception, granting a second even if one's held).

Effect when spent: every 6 rolled in that check's pool explodes — reroll and keep adding, chaining on new 6s too. Spent at the moment the player chooses. Charmed Life modifies this: roll 1 die on spend, 5–6 keeps the flag instead of losing it.

Earned by (always a moment of change):

  • The House Leader dies — the new heir gains Destiny (or keeps it, if already held)
  • A house overtakes the current #1 in some ranking
  • A house becomes the new lowest-ranked
  • Calamity compensation (module-specific)
  • Resolving a vendetta against another house
  • Winning the crown, by any path

Related but separate: whenever a character is directly involved in any trigger above, they may also swap one existing trait for a freshly flipped one — automatic, regardless of whether a new Destiny flag was actually granted.
