Playing a Decade

Each decade runs through five phases in order. Tasks assigned in Phase 5 of one decade resolve at the top of Phase 1 of the next decade — there's a full decade of in-fiction time between assigning a task and learning its outcome.

Phase 1 — Resolution

Opens the decade by settling everything set in motion last decade.

1Run the Fate Check for births and deaths — see Birth & Death.
2Check task outcomes. Every task assigned last decade resolves now (STATIC or COMPETITIVE, as the action calls for) — except a task whose relative died in step 1, which auto-fails, no roll. A relative whose task was War gets one additional death check right now, once that War's outcome is known.
3Apply trait changes. Whenever a character is directly involved in a Destiny-triggering event, they may swap one existing trait for a newly flipped one — see Destiny for the full trigger list.

Phase 2 — Scene Declaration

Every House writes exactly 1 scene intent for the decade on a notecard — who they want a scene with, and what they're attempting. All cards flip simultaneously; nothing stays hidden once flipped. One scene per House, flat, regardless of player count.

If two Houses' declared scenes conflict, house order sets priority — whoever's earlier in the order gets their scene as declared; the conflicting scene is bumped or re-narrated around it.

Phase 3 — Scene Resolution

Since nothing is hidden after declaration, every declared scene can run in parallel around the table — no single-adjudicator bottleneck. Scenes are freeform by default — a Scene plays out narratively, and only calls for a dice-pool check at the moment something concrete is actually being risked (a poisoning attempt triggers Espionage, a betrothal negotiation triggers Diplomacy). Pure conversation needs no roll at all.

Phase 4 — Kingdom Scene

The King's player reveals the decade's big public moment, drawing from the events deck — or, if the throne is vacant, any player does. Suit = category, rank = quality (low→high = bad→good). If any House has Legend 10+, that House draws extra cards (one per every 10 Legend) and keeps the lowest. What the kept card does to the Doom Clock depends on the chosen Calamity module — see Calamity Modules.

The Doom Clock's value is public — tracked openly at the table, never hidden. This is also where a declared Revolt resolves, if one was declared this same decade.

Decade 1 special case: the throne cannot leave this Kingdom Scene still vacant. If nobody bid, any player flips one card per House right now — highest is forced onto the throne (redraw ties).

The King's per-turn powers happen here too: the King must grant 1 resource from the unclaimed pool, may then redistribute one resource between Houses, may confer a moniker (pure fluff, no roll, capped at one per Leader ever), and may move 1 House's position in the house order.

Phase 5 — Task Assignment

Players delegate relatives to a specific action within one of the six task categories. The House Leader can be assigned like any other relative. These tasks resolve next decade, in Phase 1.

  • War can only target a ring-adjacent House — every other task type is unrestricted by geography.
  • One Task per relative per decade — no double-booking across two Tasks.
  • A relative in this decade's Scene can also take a Task the same decade — but at most 2 relatives per House may be double-booked this way (Scene + Task) per decade.

A Worked Example

Four Houses, five decades, hitting every major system at least once — Static and Competitive checks, the Fate Check, War as a standing state, Broker Peace, Destiny, Succession, Kingdom Scene, moniker conferral, and a full Revolt.

The table

House Ashvale — our point-of-view House, followed closely below. House Corrow, House Talvane, and House Brenmoor — the other three, referenced by name when they interact with Ashvale. House order: Ashvale → Corrow → Talvane → Brenmoor → back to Ashvale (so Ashvale's ring-neighbors are Corrow and Brenmoor).

Setting up House Ashvale

  • Archetype/Succession: Noble House (baseline), Primogeniture.
  • Traits: chosen — Great Warriors (+2 War / -1 Diplomacy), Frugal (+2 Trade / -1 War); rolled — Ancestral Seat (+2 Stewardship / -1 Magic), Magical (Magic-conversion trigger widens to any face card / -1 Diplomacy).
  • Resources: Land 5 / Manpower 4 / Wealth 3 (12 points). Honor 0, Legend 0, Piety 0, Cunning 3.
  • Leader — Birth Deal: 10d6 rolled (0 automatic hits at Legend 0) → 4 successes → 4 traits flipped on the Character Trait table. No Ace of Spades or face card came up, so no Magic conversion. Result: Lord Aldric Ashvale, age 3.
  • Family: spouse Lady Wren Ashvale (Birth Deal, age 3), and one child, Elenor Ashvale (age 0) — no Fecund, so just the one.

The other three Houses complete the identical procedure.

Decade 1

Phase 1. No prior decade exists yet, so no task outcomes to check. The Fate Check still runs — nobody dies, no births land yet (Aldric & Wren's marriage is too fresh).

Phase 2–3. Ashvale writes a notecard approaching Corrow about an alliance, then it resolves as Forge Alliance (Diplomacy, Static, Manpower+Cunning). Pool = 4+3 = 7, target = round(7÷3)+1 = 3. Rolled 6,6,5,4,2,3,1 → three 5–6s → succeeds. Ashvale and Corrow now get +2 dice on any task they take together.

Phase 4. Nobody bid for the throne, so Decade 1's forced draw happens: Ashvale 7, Corrow Q, Talvane K, Brenmoor 3. Talvane's King (K) forces them onto the throne — +10 Legend, +1 Destiny, immediately. Their Legend just hit 10, so they draw 1 extra events-deck card and keep the lowest. King's powers: grants 1 Wealth to Brenmoor, declines the rest this decade.

Phase 5. Ashvale assigns Lord Aldric to Develop Territory (Stewardship, Land+Manpower) — resolves next decade.

Decade 2

Phase 1. Fate Check clean. Aldric's Develop Territory resolves: pool = 5+4 = 9, target = 4. Rolled 6,5,3,6,2,4,5,1,6 → five 5–6s → succeeds. Ashvale claims 1 Wealth from the pool (3→4).

Phase 4. King Talvane grants 1 Land to their own house (allowed — the mandatory grant isn't restricted from benefiting the King's own House).

Phase 5. Ashvale declares War against ring-adjacent Corrow (Manpower+Wealth) — War resolves immediately, not next decade. Ashvale's pool = 4+4 = 8, Corrow's = 3+3 = 6. Both under 14, so the Competitive band width is 1. Rolled: Ashvale 4 successes, Corrow 1 — margin 3, decisive win. Ashvale captures 1 Land and 1 Manpower. Lord Aldric takes his War Death Check (age 2, Ace band, ~7.7%) — survives. The War is now a standing state, continuing automatically each decade until someone Brokers Peace.

Decade 3

Phase 1. Aldric & Wren's couple-flip lands Queen–King — a birth occurs, new child via Birth Deal. War with Corrow auto-resolves again — Ashvale wins again.

Phase 2–3. Corrow declares Broker Peace with Ashvale (no roll) — the standing War ends. Corrow gains +1 Honor, and this counts as "resolving a vendetta" — Corrow's Leader gains Destiny.

Phase 4. King Talvane, moved by the reconciliation, confers a moniker: "Corrow the Peacemaker" — pure fluff, no roll.

Phase 5. Ashvale assigns Lady Wren to Invoke Faith to start building Piety.

Decade 4

Phase 1. Lord Aldric is now age 6 (60–69, Ace-4 band, ~30.8%) — his flip lands in-band. Lord Aldric dies. Per Primogeniture, his eldest living child Elenor (now adult) inherits as the new Leader. This is a Destiny-triggering event — since Ashvale's house didn't already hold one, Elenor gains Destiny. Directly involved, she may also swap one existing trait for a freshly flipped one.

Decade 5

Phase 2. Elenor, now Leader, declares a Revolt against King Talvane.

Phase 4 — Revolt resolves. Alliances drawn live: Ashvale anchors the challenger bloc, Corrow joins them (remembering past generosity). Talvane's own house auto-anchors the loyalist bloc; Brenmoor backs Talvane. Challenger pool = Ashvale + Corrow (Manpower+Wealth each); loyalist pool = Talvane + Brenmoor. Binary win/lose — the challenger bloc rolls more successes. Elenor Ashvale becomes the new Queen — +10 Legend permanent (Ashvale already holds Destiny from Decade 4, so no change there, capped at one).

Talvane's dethroned house pays the failure cost: 1/4 of Land, Manpower, and Wealth (rounded down) to the unclaimed pool, and every relative committed to their bloc's contribution takes a death check. Elenor extends Mercy to Brenmoor (who only backed Talvane out of habit) — their cost is waived. Talvane's own house still pays in full.

What this trace covers

Setup, Static and Competitive checks at both small and larger pool sizes, the Fate Check (birth and death), War as a standing state, Broker Peace, Destiny's full trigger list and the trait-swap effect, Succession, Kingdom Scene's Legend/Doom-Clock draw, moniker conferral, and a full Revolt with Mercy. Every major system in the game, touched at least once.
